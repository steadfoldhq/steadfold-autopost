## Fully Funded: "Freedom, not homework"

**The feeling.** The exhale you feel when a door opens onto the sea. Money as *room*: time, air, a horizon. Then the calm
voice of someone who shows you the plain math and the catch, and never sells you anything.

**The visual world.** Two rooms. The *inside* is deep cobalt (`#16206B` → near-black): quiet, enclosed, the everyday.
The *outside* is real golden-hour water, graded warm (coral highlights, cobalt shadows). Type is white Inter Display for
numbers and Instrument Serif italics in soft coral for the feeling. Cream cards (`#F7F1E5`) hold the math, like a page
handed to you on the beach.

**Signature shots.**
- *The door*: a closed cobalt door, light leaking from the crack and the floor, dust in the light. It swings open on the bar.
- *The horizon number*: a figure rolling up over open water, with a coral "EXAMPLE" stamp.
- *The plain-math card*: a cream card rising over blurred sea, one row at a time.
- *The catch, indoors*: we step back into cobalt for the catch, so honesty has its own room.

**Sound design.** Room tone and silence → a latch click → the door's air rush and a wave arriving as strings swell.
A single felt-piano note on each 3-second bar. The music *breathes out* under the catch and swells back for the final line.
Whooshes are soft, never zippy.

**The hook frame.** Cobalt dark, a glowing door, and three lines readable with the sound off:
"100 a month. *From birth to 18.* …then never touch it."

**Three hero concepts.**
1. **The Door** (built). Door opens onto the sea; ≈2,036,000 at 65 (8% a year, example); the plain math; 6/8/10%;
   the catch (returns swing, fees, inflation: ≈409,000 in today's money); "The door is time. Open it early."
2. **Two Windows.** Split screen of the same ocean, one starting at birth and one at 30, each with its own counter.
   The left fills with light while the right is still dawn. Same deposits, different mornings.
3. **The Long Exposure.** One shot of a tide coming in, sped up into a 12-second long exposure, with a running
   balance in the corner. "Nothing happened for years. Then everything did." The catch arrives as the tide pulls back.

## Locked Hour: "Calm power"

**The feeling.** The moment the noise cuts out and you're the only one in the room who knows what they're doing next.
Not a checklist: a *drop*, from a city's roar to a still lake.

**The visual world.** Act one: night traffic from above, cool and over-busy, white notification cards piling up.
Act two: a misty lake at sunrise, soft and warm, with Locked Hour's butter-yellow zine type (black ink outline, hard
offset shadow) sitting on the calm like stickers on a window. Pink highlighter only for the problem, butter yellow for
the fix. Black is ink, never a background.

**Signature shots.**
- *The pile-up*: notifications stacking and buzzing over traffic.
- *The slit*: the city tears open on a horizontal butter-yellow slit into the lake.
- *The ring*: a keyhole clock and a butter ring drawing itself round a 60:00 that starts to tick.
- *The zine sticker words*: "another room", "Not three", "Pings off" as yellow blocks with a hard shadow.

**Sound design.** City roar, horns and pings layered for 3 seconds, the bed held low under them. Then a hard cut to
near-silence: a warm pad, a slow piano and a heartbeat kick. One soft "ding" when the timer starts. The pings return,
faint, on the loop, so the end flows back into the hook.

**The hook frame.** Blurred traffic, white type: "Busy all day. *Nothing finished.*" (pink highlighter), and the first
notification already sliding in.

**Three hero concepts.**
1. **Mute the World** (built). Chaos → slit → lake. "Lock one hour." 60:00 ring. Phone: another room. One task.
   Door closed. Mind wanders? Bring it back. "The world can wait an hour."
2. **The Volume Knob.** A real dial turning down: every notch removes one source of noise from the frame (tabs, phone,
   chat) until only a desk lamp and one sentence remain.
3. **Minute 1 vs Minute 45.** The same lake shot at two moments, split top and bottom: top restless (wind, ripples,
   fidgeting cursor), bottom glass-still. "It gets quiet if you stay."

## Hourback: "A sitcom moment"

**The feeling.** The laugh of recognition, then the little "huh, that's clever". The viewer is the main character of a
feel-good comedy, and AI is the sidekick who rescues the episode. Never a tool ad; never "paste this prompt" as the hook.

**The visual world.** A warm sitcom living room built in code: peach wall with soft periwinkle stripes, a window, a lamp
glow, an indigo sofa. A phone that's always a little too full. Poppins headlines, the yellow sitcom caption box with an
ink shadow, plum and cream for "the fix" cards. Pixel transitions for the reveal (Hourback's own).

**Signature shots.**
- *The overflowing phone*: a group chat that buzzes and climbs.
- *The freeze frame*: the record scratch, the picture goes sepia, the yellow caption says "Yep. That's me."
- *The typing prompt card*: one line typed, not pasted.
- *The answer card*: three lines that land one by one, then the stamp ("147 messages. 2 jobs.").

**Sound design.** Funky sitcom bass and stabs, phone buzzes, then *the music stops dead* on a record scratch. A
narrator (realistic AI voice, labelled in the caption) says the line everyone knows. The groove comes back on the pixel
reveal. A bright ding on the payoff stamp.

**The hook frame.** The living room, a phone already full of family messages, and "147 unread *in the family chat.*"

**Three hero concepts.**
1. **Freeze Frame** (built). Family chat explosion → record scratch → "Yep. That's me." → copy the chat, take out names
   and numbers → "What do I need to do, and by when?" → 147 messages, 2 jobs.
2. **The Cold Open.** Sitcom title card "Season 1, Episode 4: The Permission Slip", a 3-shot comedy of a lost school form,
   and AI as the neighbour who walks in with the answer. End on the theme-song sting.
3. **Laugh Track Test.** Three everyday disasters (the 40-email thread, the recipe for 12 when you're 2, the gift you forgot)
   each with a canned "oooh", then the one-line fix and an "aaah" of relief.

## Littlefold: "A warm hug at 3 am"

**The feeling.** Being found in the dark. The parent who is awake, tired and alone learns they aren't alone, and gets
one small, gentle, official-source thing that helps. No pressure, no fear, no perfect parents.

**The visual world.** Real rain on a night window, graded warm (peach street lights, berry shadows). A code-built
night skyline in Littlefold's berry (`#7A2149`) where windows light up in peach, one by one. A soft lamp glow that grows.
Lora with italic blush words, cream Sherbet cards for the official tip. Never a real child on screen.

**Signature shots.**
- *3:07*: a big clock with a blinking colon over rain on glass.
- *The lit windows*: a city of windows warming up, one at a time.
- *The lamp*: a peach glow rising from the bottom of the frame.
- *The NHS card*: a cream Sherbet card, "Make night feel like night."

**Sound design.** Soft rain at the window under the whole Reel. A lullaby on felt piano, music box and warm cello that
lifts a little at the end, like morning. No whooshes louder than a breath.

**The hook frame.** Rain, warm street light, "3:07 am", and "Up again. *Just you and the quiet.*"

**Three hero concepts.**
1. **3 am, Lit Windows** (built). 3:07 → look at the lit windows → another parent, up too → you're not the only one
   awake → NHS: make night feel like night (lights low, voice soft, fed and changed, back down) → send this to someone
   up at 3 am.
2. **The Night Shift Roll Call.** Clock faces from 1 am to 5 am, each with one honest line from the night ("2:14, the
   burp that won't come") as sticky notes, ending on sunrise and the official tip.
3. **Two Lamps.** The same window, two nights apart (code-built), with a small win each time. "It doesn't feel like
   progress at 3 am. It is."
