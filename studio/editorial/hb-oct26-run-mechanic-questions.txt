===== PROMPT (verbatim) =====
My car makes a squeal when I brake. Give me 5 short questions to ask the mechanic so I understand the quote before I say yes.

===== REPLY (verbatim) =====
Here are 5 questions to ask before you say yes:

1. **"What exactly is causing the squeal?"**
   Ask them to name the cause: worn pads, glazed rotors, missing shims, or just dust or moisture. Better yet, ask to see the worn part. A good shop will show you.

2. **"How much pad life is left, and is this urgent or can it wait?"**
   Pads are often measured in millimetres. Around 3mm or less usually means replace soon. This tells you whether it's a safety issue now or something to plan for.

3. **"Do the rotors really need replacing, or can they be resurfaced or reused?"**
   Rotors are the metal discs the pads squeeze against. Replacing them roughly doubles the bill, so ask why they can't be kept, such as being too thin, warped, or deeply grooved.

4. **"Can you break the quote into parts and labour, and what grade of parts is that?"**
   This shows you where the money goes. It also tells you whether you're getting OEM (original manufacturer), premium aftermarket, or budget parts, and which kind of pad (ceramic or semi-metallic).

5. **"What warranty comes with the parts and the work, and what if it still squeals afterward?"**
   This protects you if the noise comes back next week.

**Bonus tip:** If the quote seems high, it's normal to say "thanks, let me think about it" and get a second quote. Brake jobs are one of the easiest repairs to compare between shops.
